The Holy Month

Ramadan

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar and the holiest period of the year for Muslims worldwide. It commemorates the first revelation of the Quran to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and is marked by fasting, prayer and community. Ramadan is the month of fasting where the fourth pillar of Islam is practiced. For the whole month, Muslims do not eat food or drink water from sunrise to sunset.

☽

When It Occurs

Ramadan begins and ends with the sighting of the crescent moon. Because the Islamic calendar is lunar, its dates shift approximately 10–11 days earlier each year relative to the Gregorian calendar.

📖

Laylat al-Qadr

The "Night of Power" — An important day during the last 10 days of Ramadan, although the exact date is unknown, Laylat al-Qadr is the night in Islam.

🌍

Global Observance

From Indonesia to Morocco, Turkey to the United States, Ramadan unites brings together Muslims gobally for this special time.

Daily Structure

The Rhythm of the Day

Pre-Dawn

Suhoor

Before dawn, Muslims wake up to eat a meal before they begin their fast. Suhoor has many cultral differences, but is intended to be a small meal early in the day.

Dawn to Sunset

The Fast (Sawm)

Muslims abstain from food, drink, smoking, and sexual relations from dawn until sunset. Further, during the entire month of Ramadan Muslims attempt to avoid gossip, anger, and idle behavior as a practice in Moral disipline. Muslims are meant to go above and beyond their normal moral disipline and better themselves for next year

At Sunset

Iftar

The fast is traditionally broken with dates and water, following the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ. Afterwards, families and communities come together for large meals late into the night

Night

Tarawih Prayers

Special nightly prayers performed during Ramadan. Over the month, the Quran is recited in full.

"Whoever fasts Ramadan out of faith and hoping for reward, his previous sins will be forgiven."

— Hadith reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim, attributed to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Context

Ramadan & the Five Pillars of Islam

Ramadan directly contains four of Islam's Five Pillars — the core obligations that form the foundation of Muslim life and practice.

I

Shahada — Declaration of Faith

The Shahada is the most important aspect of Islam. It goes as follows: "There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is His messenger." The Shahada is recited during every prayer and with the special focus of prayer during Ramadan, it is repeated quite often. Further, the purpose of Ramadan is to strengthen the connection between Allah and the follower in rememberance of god which is the point of the Shahada

II

Salah — Prayer

During Ramadan, Muslims are encouraged to go beyond the five daily prayers. First is of course the Tarawih. The nightly prayer is heavily encouraged for all followers to participate in alongside the community. Further prayers late into the night are recommended along with prayers along the day.

III

Zakat — Charitable Giving

Charity is amplified during Ramadan. Muslims are told to give more during Ramadan. It is more rewarded to give during Ramadan and so many give more than the 2.5% that is expected of them

IV

Sawm — Fasting

Fasting is evident in Ramadan. This is the main time most people fast and while other times it is encouraged, it is mandatory during Ramadan for all who are able.

V

Hajj — Pilgrimage

The last pillar is the Hajj. It is the only one not directly tied to Ramadan but it is still important to remember

The Festival of Breaking Fast

Eid al-Fitr

Eid al-Fitr ("the festival of breaking fast") marks the end of Ramadan. It is a day of gratitude to Allah, communal joy, and generous celebration — one of the two holiday in the Islamic calendar, alongside Eid al-Adha.

🌙

Announcement

Eid begins with the sighting of the new crescent moon at the end of Ramadan. Communitiesannounce the start of Eid, which can sometimes vary by a day across countries.

🕌

Eid Prayer

The day begins with a special congregational Eid prayer (Salat al-Eid), held in the morning at mosques or open prayer grounds. Attendance is strongly encouraged for all Muslims.

💛

Zakat al-Fitr

Zakat on Eid day is once again highly encouraged

The Celebration

How Eid is Observed

Personal & Spiritual Preparations

  • Perform a full ritual bath (Ghusl) before the Eid prayer
  • Wear new or finest clothes as a sign of gratitude
  • Eat something sweet, traditionally dates, before the prayer
  • Pay Zakat al-Fitr on behalf of the entire family
  • Recite Takbeer: "Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar, La ilaha illallah…"
  • Walk to the prayer ground if possible, returning by a different route

Community & Family

  • Exchange greetings: "Eid Mubarak!" or "Eid Sa'eed!"
  • Visit family, friends, neighbors, and the elderly
  • Share festive meals and traditional sweets
  • Give gifts and money (Eidi) to children
  • Visit graves of deceased relatives and pray for them
  • Embrace reconciliation — mend broken relationships

Around the World

Cultural Traditions

🍽️

Food & Feasting

Every culture brings its own culinary tradition to Eid. From kunafa (Middle East) to sheer khurma (South Asia), biryani (Pakistan/India), ma'amoul shortbread (Levant), and baklava (Turkey), food is a centerpiece of Ramadan.

🪔

Lights & Decoration

Streets and homes are adorned with lights, lanterns (fanous), and bunting across many Muslim-majority countries.

👗

New Clothes

Wearing new garments on Eid is a tradition symbolizing renewal, gratitude, and the spiritual freshness achieved through Ramadan.

🤲

Acts of Charity

Eid and Ramadan sees an outpouring of voluntary generosity, feeding the poor, giving gifts, and supporting community organizations.

🎉

Community Gatherings

Parks, fairgrounds, and community halls host Eid festivals with entertainment, games for children, and markets.

Three Days of Joy

Eid al-Fitr is traditionally celebrated for three days, though modern life has condensed this in many places to one or two. In many Muslim-majority countries, it is a public holiday. These days are days of enjoyment, so much so that fasting is forbidden. These are meant to be happy days.

عيد مبارك

Eid Mubarak — Blessed Celebration

May the blessings of Ramadan carry forward, and the joy of Eid be shared by all.
